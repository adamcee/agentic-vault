# Goals and Audience
*Updated 2026-09-17 with interview and competitor evidence.*

Brigade wins by making the weekly schedule fast to build on a laptop and easy to follow on a phone, for restaurants too small for enterprise tools. It earns money from a flat fee per [[concepts/location]].

## Audience
- **Buyers:** owners and general managers of independent restaurants with one to three locations and 10 to 60 staff.
- **Daily users:** managers who build the schedule on a laptop or back-office PC ([[research/manager-interview-synthesis]] [supported]).
- **Staff:** servers, cooks, and bartenders who need to see shifts and trade them from a phone, without installing an app.

## Goals
- **Pilot (by 2026-10-30):** both pilot restaurants publish every weekly schedule in Brigade for four straight weeks.
- **First revenue (by 2027-01-31):** ten paying locations at the price set in [[decisions/product/PDR-P001-flat-fee-per-location|PDR-P001]].
- **Setup:** an owner goes from sign-up to a published first schedule in one evening.

## What Success Looks Like
- Managers build a week in under an hour, down from the one to two hours most reported ([[research/manager-interview-synthesis]] [supported]).
- Shift trades move out of group texts and into Brigade, where the schedule stays accurate.
- Owners renew without a sales call.

## Out of Scope
- Chains with more than three locations.
- Payroll, tips, and wage calculation, as set by clause P3 of [[architecture/CONSTITUTION]].
- Point-of-sale and time-clock integrations until after first revenue.

## Positioning
Generic scheduling apps are built phone-first for managers and mostly charge per employee ([[research/competitor-feature-scan]] [emerging]). Brigade puts the manager on a desktop grid, the staff member on a phone, and the price on the location.
