# An In-Depth Exploration of SMS Provider Options for Brigade

## Overview

As Brigade continues on its exciting journey toward a full launch, it is crucial to take a step back and delve into the question of which SMS provider will best support its notification needs going forward. In this document, I have carefully analyzed three providers, referred to here as Provider A, Provider B, and Provider C, in order to offer a robust and well-rounded perspective. It is worth noting at the outset that each provider brings its own unique set of strengths and weaknesses to the table, and the right choice will ultimately depend on a variety of considerations.

## Background and Context

As previously discussed, the pilot sent 603 messages across two restaurants in its first two weeks. This volume, while modest, nonetheless highlights the importance of selecting a provider that can seamlessly scale alongside Brigade as it grows. Needless to say, reliability is absolutely paramount in the restaurant context, where a missed shift alert could potentially lead to understaffing during a busy service.

## Provider A

Provider A is arguably one of the more established options, and it offers what appears to be a comprehensive and feature-rich API with extensive documentation. Its per-message pricing seems to be somewhat higher than the alternatives, though it could be argued that this premium may be justified by its reputation for deliverability. It also appears to support two-way messaging, which might perhaps be useful in the future.

## Provider B

Provider B, on the other hand, positions itself as a more cost-effective solution, and it is interesting to note that its pricing may be noticeably lower at Brigade's current volume. That said, some users have reportedly experienced occasional delays, although it is difficult to say with certainty how representative these reports might be.

## Provider C

Provider C represents something of a middle ground, offering a balanced blend of pricing and features that could make it an attractive option for teams looking to leverage a flexible platform. Its onboarding process for business sender registration seems to be relatively straightforward, which may be a meaningful advantage for a small team.

## Recommendation

Taking all of the above into account, it seems reasonable to tentatively suggest that Provider C may potentially offer the best overall fit for Brigade at this stage, although further investigation would certainly be warranted. In conclusion, the SMS provider landscape is rich and varied, and a thoughtful, holistic evaluation will be key to unlocking long-term synergy between Brigade and its chosen partner.
