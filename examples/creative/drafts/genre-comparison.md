# A Comprehensive Comparison of Short Narrative Puzzle Games in the Genre

## Introduction

In this document, I will delve into a detailed and multifaceted comparison of four short narrative puzzle games that the team played over the past two weeks, drawing on Mara's and Teo's play notes. It is worth noting that the genre is a rich and diverse landscape, and as such any comparison must necessarily be somewhat limited in scope, nuanced, and provisional.

This section covers the background and context. As previously discussed, Low Tide sits within a crowded and ever-evolving space, so it is crucial to understand how comparable games approach hints, choices, and length.

## The Games Compared

For the purposes of this analysis, the games are referred to as Game A through Game D in order to maintain focus on design patterns rather than specific titles.

- **Game A** is a lighthouse-themed puzzle game of roughly two hours that arguably offers a seamless blend of story and puzzle, with a hint system that appears to be hidden inside a character's diary.
- **Game B** is a somewhat longer game, perhaps four hours, featuring branching choices and multiple endings, which the team felt may have potentially diluted the impact of its central story.
- **Game C** is a short, highly atmospheric experience with no hints at all, which could be seen as either a strength or a weakness depending on one's perspective.
- **Game D** is a puzzle-heavy title with a robust, three-tier hint button system that the team found useful but which, it might be argued, interrupted the mood.

## Key Themes and Insights

When we delve deeper, a fascinating tapestry of patterns begins to emerge. The following table shows how each game handles hints.

| Game | Hints | Endings | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | In-world diary | One | ~2 hours |
| B | Button | Three | ~4 hours |
| C | None | One | ~1.5 hours |
| D | Three-tier button | One | ~3 hours |

It is important to note that the games which embedded their hints in the world, such as Game A, were generally perceived by both team members as leveraging the narrative to support the player in a way that felt organic and holistic, though of course this is only one interpretation among many.

Furthermore, it could be argued that the synergy between in-world hints and a single ending represents a testament to focused design, which may be highly relevant for Low Tide going forward.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, this comparison suggests that Low Tide may potentially benefit from exploring in-world hints, although further research would of course be needed. Ultimately, there are many valid approaches, and I hope this overview has been helpful in shedding light on the rich and complex landscape of the genre.
