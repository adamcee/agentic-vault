# Comprehensive Scope Estimate Report for Low Tide

## Introduction

In this report, I will delve into a comprehensive and thorough analysis of the overall scope of the Low Tide project, with the goal of providing a robust and holistic understanding of whether the current branching narrative structure is feasible given the team's available resources. It is worth noting that scope estimation is an inherently complex and nuanced endeavor, and as such the figures presented here should be considered approximate, indicative, and subject to change.

As previously discussed, the team consists of two individuals, and it is important to keep in mind that small teams often face unique challenges in the ever-evolving landscape of independent game development.

## Analysis

When we delve deeper into the numbers, a fascinating picture begins to emerge. The branching structure, with its three distinct and unique endings, requires approximately 41 scenes, around 95,000 words of dialogue, and roughly 58 backgrounds. By contrast, a linear structure would require approximately 24 scenes, 40,000 words, and 30 backgrounds.

Industry-standard benchmarks suggest that indie studios typically complete around 70% of their originally planned content, which may be relevant here. This is a testament to the challenges inherent in creative production.

Given 30 weeks remaining and 20 art hours per week, the artist has approximately 600 hours available. The branching background workload would therefore come to approximately 522 hours, which, when combined with an estimated 150 hours of character animation, would likely exceed available capacity, although it is possible that efficiencies could be found.

Similarly, the writing workload for the branching structure would take approximately 63 weeks at current rates, which is significantly more than the 30 weeks available. This is a crucial finding.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, it seems reasonable to suggest that the team may wish to consider whether a linear structure might potentially offer a more seamless path to completion, while still leveraging the strengths of the current narrative design. Ultimately, the decision rests with the team, and there are many valid perspectives to consider. I hope this analysis has been helpful in illuminating the tapestry of factors at play.
