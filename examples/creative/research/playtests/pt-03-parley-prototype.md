# Playtest 3: Parley Prototype

Seven of eight players preferred parley to the earlier turn-based encounters. The tone complaint from [[research/playtests/pt-02-greybox-encounters]] came back with mostly new players, so this doc is tagged `[supported]`.

## Method
- **Build.** The build reused the pt-02 greybox route in two versions. Version A kept the turn-based fights; version B replaced them with parley, where Wren talks, offers an item from the satchel, or shines the lantern.
- **Players.** Eight adults (P1–P8) took part. Two had played pt-02; six were new, recruited from the devlog newsletter.
- **Sessions.** Sessions ran from 2026-06-15 to 2026-06-18. Each player played 15 minutes of each version, with half starting on A and half on B.
- **Questions.** After both versions, players picked the version they preferred and said why. Ines asked how each version fit the game's tone.

## Findings
- **Parley won.** 7/8 preferred parley. P7 preferred the fights because "parley never felt dangerous."
- **The tone finding repeated.** 6/8 said the turn-based fights felt out of place in a story about delivering letters, echoing the 5/6 in pt-02.
- **Opened letters fed parley.** 6/8 opened at least one sealed letter and used what they read to talk their way past a creature. Ines saw players reread letters before a parley.
- **Parley was faster.** A parley took about 90 seconds on average, against about four minutes for a fight.

### Player Quotes
- **P2:** "I read the hound's owner's letter, so I knew its name. I just called it home."
- **P4:** "This feels like the same game as the letters now."
- **P5:** "Shining the lantern at the moth thing felt rude. I offered it thread instead."
- **P7:** "I miss the fights a little. Nothing here can hurt me."

## Limits
- **The preference is one session.** Only the tone finding has a second session behind it; the 7/8 preference has not been repeated.
- **Order effects.** Fifteen minutes per version is short, and players may favor whichever version felt newer.
- **Greybox only.** Neither version had art, music, or finished writing.
