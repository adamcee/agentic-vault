# PDR-P001: Letters Are the Quests

## Context
Night Post started from one image: a courier walking a dark forest with a satchel of other people's mail. The team needed a quest structure that fits that image and that one writer can produce.

## Decision
The player is Wren, the newest courier of the Night Post, and every quest is a letter ([[world/characters/wren]], [[world/factions/the-night-post]]). Each delivery reveals a relationship, a secret, or a debt between Hollowmere's villages, and each night runs from picking up letters at dusk to returning by dawn ([[architecture/game-design-overview]]).

## Alternatives Considered
- **A standard quest log.** Characters hand out tasks in person. Players know the format, but quests stop feeling like private mail.
- **Letters as collectibles beside regular quests.** Letters stay as flavor, and the writing splits across two quest formats.
- **Deliveries as a side job between story chapters.** Deliveries would become filler between the parts the team cares about.

## Consequences
- The writing load concentrates in letters, so letter length becomes a design budget.
- Every system serves the delivery loop: routes ([[concepts/moon-shift]]), time ([[concepts/lantern-oil]]), and carrying capacity ([[concepts/satchel-weight]]).
- The fantasy raises the question of opening other people's mail, settled in [[decisions/product/PDR-P008-sealed-letters-can-be-opened|PDR-P008]].
- **Reweave 2026-04-24:** strengthened by [[research/playtests/pt-01-paper-prototype]] [emerging]: with a paper map and printed letters, the letter-opening choice was the most discussed moment for all 5 players.
