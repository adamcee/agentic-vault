# Business Goals: Can One Van Pay a Full-Time Wage?

Curbside Cycles succeeds in 2026 if one van, working four days a week, earns enough to pay the founder a full-time wage after van and parts costs. The founder decides by 2026-12-15 whether to keep going full time, using the targets below.

## Audience
The audience is daily bike commuters who work at sites with a bike room or covered parking, served at their workplace ([[decisions/product/PDR-P001-workplace-commuter-focus|PDR-P001]]). Weekend home visits are a side line, not the core.

## Why the Gap Exists
No local shop repairs bikes on site, and every shop keeps a bike for two days or more ([[research/local-price-scan]] [emerging]). Most commuters surveyed have put off a repair for that reason ([[research/commuter-survey-2026-09]] [emerging]).

## Targets for the 2026-12-15 Decision
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| [[concepts/van-day|Van days]] worked | 4 a week |
| Revenue per van day | $350 average over the last eight weeks |
| Members | 30 by 2026-12-01 |
| Host workplaces | 6 with written permission |
| Monthly costs (van, insurance, fuel, tools, booking tool) | Under $1,300 |

At these targets, the van brings in about $6,000 a month. That leaves roughly $3,800 for the founder after parts and running costs.

## What Failure Looks Like
If revenue per van day stays under $250 through November, the founder returns to shop work and runs the van on weekends only.

> **Reweave 2026-10-05 (weakened):** The pilot earned $243 per van day, below the $350 target and at the failure line ([[research/pilot-month-results]] [emerging]). The membership in [[decisions/product/PDR-P004-membership-plus-per-visit|PDR-P004]] is the main response, and the gap is flagged for the founder.
