# PDR-P003: Lease a Small Storefront Workshop (Rejected)

## Context
A small storefront unit near downtown came up for lease at $1,800 a month on a three-year term. A workshop would let the founder take wheel builds and overhauls the van cannot handle, and it would add walk-in trade.

## Decision
Rejected. Curbside Cycles will not lease premises before demand is proven, for four reasons:
- **The fixed cost is too high.** The lease would roughly double monthly costs before the pilot has shown how many riders will book.
- **It breaks the one-van rule.** [[architecture/CONSTITUTION]] P1 holds off premises until the van runs at 80% or more of capacity for eight weeks.
- **It pulls against the market choice.** [[decisions/product/PDR-P001-workplace-commuter-focus|PDR-P001]] brings the repair to the rider, while a storefront asks the rider to come in.
- **Walk-in trade is seasonal.** Spring brings most of a city shop's repair volume, so a lease signed in autumn starts in the slow months [unsourced].

## Alternatives Considered
- **Rent a shared workshop bench by the day** for the occasional overhaul. This became the fallback for jobs the van cannot do.
- **Turn down overhaul jobs entirely.** This loses good customers for the sake of a few hours of bench time.

## Consequences
- Overhauls and wheel builds go to a rented bench day, booked only when two or more jobs are waiting.
- Revisit this record when the van meets the P1 capacity test or a host workplace offers indoor space at no rent.
